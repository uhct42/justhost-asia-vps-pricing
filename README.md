## Three kinds of JustHost discount code

**First-cycle codes.** BDAY40 today; TS50, WORLD6 and HOTSS50 previously. These usually have the biggest number and the shortest reach. They also tend to carry minimum term requirements: WORLD6 only worked on 6-month-plus billing, HOTSS50 only on the first billing period of a new order.

**Recurring codes.** JUST21 today; TS35, WORLD30X, WORLD20X and the AMS3 recurring codes previously. Smaller headline number, permanent effect. These are the ones to take if the server is going to stay up for a year or more.

**Location codes.** JustHost names data centres in most promotions and limits the code to that list. May's sale covered Tokyo, Singapore, Los Angeles, San Jose, Seattle, Chicago and Toronto; the Sofia codes covered Sofia only; USA25 covered seven US cities; the AMS3 codes covered Amsterdam only. The list changes every month, and a code that worked in one city in June may do nothing in November.

Two hard limits sit on top of all three: plan scope and no stacking. The Tokyo/Singapore codes covered the six standard plans and not the promo tier. The Sofia codes explicitly excluded the configurator. The anniversary pair is the exception on scope, covering every VPS plan including custom builds.

**Testing the network for a month.** VPS Promo, $1.99, 1 core and 512 MB on a 10 Mbit port. Ten megabits works out to roughly 4 TB a month of unmetered traffic, which is plenty for testing and useless for anything that needs to push real bandwidth. Third-party write-ups also report that ports 25, 465 and 587 are blocked on this tier, so don't plan on running mail from it.

**Two cores and 2 GB.** Alpha Centauri at $8.99. There's no Special listing for a 2-core 2 GB box, so this is the one tier where the standard price is the price.

**Four GB.** Vega Special at $7.79 is the value pick: 2 cores, 80 GB NVMe, 500 Mbit. Capella at $18.30 doubles the cores and adds 40 GB of disk for more than twice the money. Bright Star at $9.80 offers 4 cores but only 30 GB, which suits CPU-bound work more than storage.

**Eight GB and up.** Yildun Special at $16.20 covers most multi-service setups. Betelgeuse Special at $26.40 and Altair Special at $47.40 handle heavier loads, and there's no reason to pay the standard tier's $27.94 for 8 GB while the Special is in stock.

## Storage type, IPv6 and the other price levers

The same core count costs different amounts depending on the disk. On the entry configuration (1 core, 512 MB, 5 GB), HDD is $3.25, SSD is $3.90 and NVMe is $4.22 per month. NVMe is the default across the main plans, and the price gap here is small enough that HDD only makes sense if you specifically want spinning disks.

IPv6-only versions of the plans cost less, because they come without an IPv4 address. The VPS page starts IPv6-only servers at $1.60 against $2.60 for dual-stack. Whether a given plan can gain an IPv4 address later is worth confirming with support before you commit: third-party price lists put an additional IPv4 at $3.31/month, which would erase most of the saving. If your workload talks to IPv4-only endpoints, take the dual-stack plan.

"Unmetered" here means traffic isn't counted, but the port speed is capped. The page translates each port into a monthly volume: 10 Mbit is about 4 TB, 100 Mbit about 31 TB, 300 Mbit about 93 TB, and 750 Mbit about 232 TB.

## What bites after you order

**No automatic backups.** Third-party reviews and the provider's own feature list both point the same way — you supply your own backup routine. For a $3 server that's a reasonable trade; just have a plan before the disk fails, not after.

**The refund window is short.** Third-party write-ups report 15 days. Confirm the current terms on the order page, and use the window to actually test the location you picked.

**You can move servers yourself.** Location and IP switching happen in the control panel, with up to 50 free IP changes per VPS according to third-party documentation. That's genuinely useful if you're testing which region gives acceptable latency, and it's the feature most often praised in reviews.

**Support is uneven.** Reviews collected on third-party sites split hard: some report replies within ten minutes, others describe two-day waits and unhelpful chatbot sessions. Treat support as a bonus rather than a guarantee.

**Terms-of-service risk on VPN use.** The same review roundups describe accounts terminated for vaguely worded terms violations, including one case where a year-long VPS was deleted after two weeks of VPN use. Russian locations come up specifically, with services blocked by local regulators. If your use case sits anywhere near that line, don't put anything you can't rebuild on a single cheap VPS.

**Where the price lands after year one.** Without a recurring code, renewals revert to list price. That's the whole argument for JUST21 over BDAY40 if the server is going to live past a couple of months.
